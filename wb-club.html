Growing the WB Club subscription from 2.2M to 5.3M members at Wildberries

Role
Senior Product Designer
Company
Wildberries
Scope
iOS, Android
Duration
May 2025 – Jun 2026
Team
5 PMs, 2 product owners, 2 engineering teams, 3 analysts; core design team
Contributions
2.2M → 5.3M subscribers in a year · New VIP tier for top 1M buyers · 10 product streams, 18 entry points

The problem

WB Club is the paid subscription of Wildberries, a marketplace with 150M buyers. Members order more often and spend more, so the business needed the Club to grow: more members, higher conversion to subscription, and more members staying past the trial.

When I joined, most buyers never met the Club. It had a few entry points and one outdated page that only ~4% of buyers opened in a month, and buyers who had already decided still had to get through a four-step paywall.

My role

I owned the Club’s design end to end: entry points, plan pages, purchase, onboarding, renewal, cancellation and new services, from seller promos to a charity program and partner offers. Every major change was A/B tested with the analysts.

1. Making the WB Club part of every product stream

The benefits became the entry points: 18 of them across ten streams.

Designing entry points across the app rested on my key hypothesis: the Club’s benefits should reach buyers at the moment they matter. Where the Club changes an offer, such as a price, a delivery or a storage period, the entry point shows the real value the buyer would get as a member. Everywhere else, it should present the Club’s main perks.

The approach worked, with one surprise: the best-converting entry point was the simplest one, a toggle on the second step of checkout that shows only the main perks.

Beyond its own page, all the entry points live inside other teams’ screens, so the hard part was earning space there. Each of the ten streams had its own priorities, and every entry point was a negotiation. We worked through it with PMs, researchers, designers and stream stakeholders over many iterations, and validated the results in A/B tests with analysts.

2. One-click purchase instead of the paywall

Join now subscribes the buyer in one tap, and conversion to subscription rose sharply.

Working on conversion to subscription with the team, we saw many buyers drop off on the paywall. Plans, price, terms and payment method stood between a buyer who had already decided and the subscription.

I explored several ways to shorten the flow. My key hypothesis here was that if buyers could join in one tap with their saved payment method, conversion would rise without more early cancellations. After several rounds with product, legal and stakeholders, we tested the idea in A/B cycles, with updated terms and clear joining controls.

A failed payment now moves the subscription to an inactive status while the system retries, instead of losing the member. As a result, conversion to subscription rose by ~31% while guardrails stayed in the normal range.

3. Give members reasons to stay

Retention ran from the start, alongside growth.

Working on retention, I focused on three areas: delivering more value to members, converting trial users into paying ones, and improving the cancellation flow.

More value, made visible

Members stay when they understand the Club’s value, stay engaged and see the product evolve. A new onboarding introduces the perks right after joining, and a savings widget keeps a running total of what the Club has saved them. I also launched a steady flow of new benefits and partner offers, each announced through notifications, giving members a reason to keep coming back.

From trial to paid

Most members joined with a trial, which made the first full payment the moment of truth: only then could we see how much buyers truly valued the Club. A new delayed-benefit mechanic, which unlocks a perk after one to two months, gave members a concrete reason to stay past the trial and improved trial-to-paid conversion by 4%.

A pause instead of a goodbye

Freezing, a new step in the five-step cancellation flow, lets members who are about to leave put the Club on hold instead. It raised the save rate by 7%.

Over the year, first-month retention grew by 16%.

4. A new tier for the top buyers

VIP gives the top 1M buyers a free tier they keep by staying active.

Once the Shopping tier was growing, the next question was the buyers who matter most to the marketplace: the top 1M by spend. Instead of selling them another subscription, we designed a tier they earn for free.

The key hypothesis was that if they could earn it through what they already do, they would join with no price barrier, and keeping the status each month would grow their spending power on the platform.

VIP renews as long as members meet three conditions on spending and pickups, so staying in becomes a small game they want to keep winning.

The hard part was getting this segment to join at all. We invited them through notifications and promotions, and switched all 18 Club entry points to offer VIP instead of Shopping to buyers who met the conditions.

Its stronger benefits also give the ~2M buyers close to qualifying a reason to reach it. As a result, about 37% of the top buyers joined in its first year.

Design library

I built the Club’s components on the core design system, refining each with the core team round after round until it fit and still felt like the WB Club. With illustrators and UI writers, I kept the visuals and tone of voice consistent with the app. One set now covers ten streams, both plans and eight statuses.

Learnings

Distribution beats a better page.
The redesigned page mattered, but the Club grew fastest once its benefits showed up where buyers already were: cart, item page, orders.

Sometimes the best fix for a step is removing it.
We didn’t redesign the paywall; we replaced it with one tap and turned legal’s requirement into a single line under the button.

In a product that lives in other teams’ screens, design is negotiation.
Each of the ten streams agreed to an entry point once it saw what the Club gave its own buyers, not what it gave the Club.

Eligible doesn’t mean joined.
Even a free tier needed an invitation plan; the top buyers came in through notifications and entry points that spoke to them as VIP.
